# Edward's surfaces: the pulse, the widget, the /screen stage, the owner's Chrome

The places Edward shows himself and acts on the machine: the expression pulse that decides what his surfaces show, the iPhone widget, the `/screen` stage (a page on the host Mac's monitor, or any display you open it on), and his hands in the owner's Chrome through the Edward extension, which are off unless you turn them on.

## The expression pulse

(`backend/services/expression_service.py`)

- The pulse is `mind_service.wake("surfaces", …)` ([mind.md](mind.md)): Edward takes one ordinary turn in the long-lived conversation `edward-surfaces` with a compact signal pack (recent messages, presence, the pet board, the calendar, what the surfaces show, his last decisions) and decides what his surfaces show: the iPhone widget (only the owner sees it), the household screen (`/hub`, shared with the house) and his own `/screen` stage. Nothing is forced; leaving things alone is a valid outcome. **Do not replace this with a deterministic composer**: the surfaces are meant to show judgment, not a menu (design decision, 2026-09-03). `widget_expression_service.compose_widget_sections` survives only as the fallback when the turn fails or the quota is out. Manual: `POST /api/widget/pulse`.
- The system prompt (`EXPRESSION_SYSTEM_PROMPT`, built by `build_expression_system_prompt`) names the owner and the rest of the household on the shared screen, and describes the `/screen` stage in one of two ways: with Chrome hands on (the stage is a tab in the owner's Chrome, websites and videos go to Chrome itself) or off (the default: the stage is the page wherever the owner opens it, and a picture or a video goes on the stage). It is rendered once at import, so it stays byte-identical between pulses and the prompt cache holds.
- **Evolution, 2026-09-14.** The first ledger health check found the pulse taking 88 % of the weekly Codex quota at ~166K input tokens per call and starving memory extraction. Three changes:
  1. **A lean turn**: ~29 surface, browser, search and read-only memory tools instead of all 107 (schemas and the manual are gated by tool name, so both shrink). Under the one toolset (2026-10-11) the pulse's limits are its `TurnPolicy` instead; `LEGACY_PULSE_TOOLS` is honoured only under `EDWARD_TOOLSET=legacy`.
  2. **A short window with a durable memory**: `PULSE_MAX_HISTORY_CHARS` (24K characters; checkpoint trimming uses the same number), and the trigger's "Your last notes" come from `ledger_events` decisions (`recent_decisions` / `decision_lines`), which survive restarts.
  3. **A cheap tick**: the cron is `*/15` (`scheduler_service.PULSE_CRON`; `is_stale_pulse_seed` retires a row on an older schedule), but `signal_parts` digests the pack, `changed_parts` names what moved, and `should_pulse` decides whether a model turn runs at all. `PulseState` is in-process (`reset_pulse_state` in tests): the first tick after a restart always pulses.
- **Change-only** (2026-10-11, the proactivity rebuild). The `*/15` tick is a code check that runs a model turn only when a surface's inputs moved (the pet board, the calendar, the widget, the household screen, the `/screen` stage, his hands, who is at the hub) or as the first pulse since start.
  - **Messages and presence are in the pack but are not changes**: they made 1,330 "changes" a week. Nor is the owner sitting down at or leaving the host Mac: the stage key is `describe_mini_screen(snap, presence=False)`, while the trigger keeps "<owner> is at this Mac right now".
  - **There are no floors**: `should_pulse(reason, changed, last_pulse_at, quota)` skips a quiet `cron` tick with "nothing changed" (no model call). The old floors (60 min by day, 180 at night) ran about 70 pulse turns a day over packs where nothing had changed; expect a handful now.
  - A non-`cron` pulse (`POST /api/widget/pulse`, an ambient noticing, the nightly's "set the morning scene") always runs, and `run_expression_pulse(reason, extra)` / `pulse_in_background(reason, extra)` put the caller's `extra` just before the closing ask. A spent quota still skips to the fallback card.
  - The wake runs under a pulse `TurnPolicy`: not the owner asking, no reaching people, no pushes (`expression_service.pulse_policy`, `accepts_kwarg`).
  - Kept: `screen_spark` and `scene_age` in the trigger (never the change key), `_quota_state` / `quota_state` (the mind imports it) and `quota_state_for`. Tests: `tests/test_pulse_change_only.py`.
- **Ambient items expire in code** (2026-10-11): with the pulse change-only, nothing re-reads a surface every hour, so a stale item must go on its own. A widget section may carry `"until": "<ISO>"` (a naive time is the owner's zone): `widget_service.live_sections` drops it once past, in `get_widget_state` (so `GET /api/widget` never serves it) and in the pulse's widget signal (so the next tick sees the widget change and recomposes). A show on the household screen or the stage that the owner did not ask for, and that names no `seconds`, expires after two hours (`hub_canvas_service.AUTONOMOUS_SHOW_SECONDS`, `default_seconds`; the owner's ask is `by="owner"`, their own turn, or a `TurnPolicy` with `owner_asked` — `screen_service.owner_asked_now`; the hub's own panel posts `shown_by="user"`), so a failed clear can never leave a kitchen alert up. Tests: `tests/test_surface_expiry.py`.

## The widget

(`backend/services/widget_service.py`, `backend/routers/widget.py`, `scriptable/`)

- An iOS home-screen widget through the Scriptable app, with token-based public access: structured data (sections with a title and items) or raw Scriptable JavaScript. **The widget token is owner-equivalent**: `POST /api/widget/chat` (the Scriptable chat, `scriptable/edward-chat.js`) runs a turn as the owner with it (`widget` is an owner source: every read, sends, pushes), and the presence routes take it too. It belongs only on the owner's own devices; `POST /api/widget/token/regenerate` replaces a leaked one. The model-facing manual assumes one large widget (`graph/tools.py`, an `ADAPT:` seam).
- **Edward's latest write wins** (2026-09-27): the Scriptable client runs `data.script` in preference to `sections`, and `update_widget_state` never cleared `script`, so a script Edward set once with `update_widget_code` hid every structured `update_widget` after it. Each of those writes (`updated_by` edward, a fresh `updated_at`) also renewed the fallback's 6-hour hold, so the fallback never replaced it either: the phone showed Friday's pet board all weekend. `update_widget_state` now sets `script = None`; `update_widget_code` still sets one, which the phone shows until the next `update_widget` (both tool docstrings say so). The pulse trigger names a running script instead of the sections it hides. Tests: `tests/test_widget_service.py`, `tests/test_expression_service.py`.
- Tables: `widget_state` (title, subtitle, theme, sections, script), `widget_tokens` (token, is_active). In the toolset the widget is `screen(widget | widget_code | widget_clear | state)`; the legacy tools are `update_widget`, `get_widget_state_tool`, `update_widget_code`, `clear_widget_code`.
- REST: `GET /api/widget?token=<token>` (public), `POST /api/widget/chat` (public, token-checked), `GET /api/widget/token`, `POST /api/widget/token/regenerate`, `POST /api/widget/refresh`, `POST /api/widget/pulse` (a manual pulse).
- **The repo's scripts carry no token** (2026-09-27): `scriptable/edward-widget.js` once had a live token hard-coded. It now ships `const TOKEN = "YOUR_TOKEN_HERE";` like `scriptable/edward-chat.js`; the phone's copy (Scriptable's iCloud folder) or the widget parameter `url|token` carries the real one. Fetch it with `GET /api/widget/token`. `tests/test_widget_service.py` fails when any `scriptable/*.js` sets `TOKEN` to anything but the placeholder (naming the file, never the value).
- The token rides the query string, so the access log redacts it (`services/access_log.py`, 2026-09-27).
- **The default card** (`widget_service._generate_default_content`, served by `GET /api/widget` only while no `widget_state` row exists; 2026-09-27): the owner's events only (`upcoming_items` leaves `today_service.PLUMBING_EVENT_TYPES` out of both "Upcoming" and the "Pending" count), with `next_fire_at` converted from naive UTC to the owner's zone (`_fire_label`, "Sep 27, 5:00 pm"), and the greeting by that zone's clock, not the host's (`greeting_for`: "Good morning, <EDWARD_OWNER_NAME>", or plain "Good morning"). `compose_widget_sections` (the fallback when the pulse fails or the quota is out) lists no events, and its subtitle still uses the host clock. The card used to print UTC as local and list the plumbing rows as upcoming. The tests pin the owner in America/Los_Angeles so a host-clock bug cannot pass on a host in another zone. Tests: `tests/test_widget_service.py`, `test_widget_expression.py`.

## The /screen stage

(`backend/services/screen_service.py`, `backend/services/screen_thoughts.py`, `backend/services/screen_window_service.py`, `backend/routers/screen.py`, `frontend/app/(display)/screen` + `display/components/ScreenLayout.tsx` → `display/components/Stage.tsx`)

- **Why** (2026-09-07): the desk monitor is Edward's whenever the owner is not using it. He treats it like the widget: full creative control on his own judgment until asked ("put that concert video on your screen"). It replaced a fixed orbit layout.
- **It works without Chrome hands.** `/screen` is an ordinary page of the app (`/display` and `/fullscreen` are aliases): open it in any browser on any display and it polls, draws the stage and plays his lines. Chrome hands (below) only add Edward raising and sizing that tab himself.
- **The stage** (2026-09-28). The 6×4 tile grid it replaced (`set_screen_layout`, first-fit packing, the orbit rings and their components, all deleted) read as a dashboard, not as Edward. /screen is now a stage he writes on: one **scene** of up to eight pieces placed freely on a 24×12-cell stage, where **he** stands on it, the **lines** he says, and his **thinking** while a wake or the pulse really runs. Over it, as before, the **show**: `show_on_screen(screen="mini")` (`mini` is the stage's historical name in the code, as in `owner_at_mini`) overlays the household kinds (image, video, alert, text, html) and `clear_screen(screen="mini")` takes it down; `screen="house"` (the default) is the hub canvas. **There is no `web` kind and no browser tile** (removed 2026-09-07): websites go to the owner's Chrome, with hands on.
- **Round 2: always his mind, and his to use** (2026-09-28, the same evening). Looking at the live stage, the ask was for it to be less deterministic: it should always show what he is thinking or dreaming, and he should feel encouraged to do something with it. What it showed instead: the owner was at the desk, so the page was in desk mode (every piece dimmed to 0.25, Edward a small bust in the corner, no thought lane), and the compose reply card printed raw markdown under a stack of bare "Thinking..." lines, while Edward ran 10–20 mind, pulse and free-time turns an hour. So: the lane is never empty while he has a thought from the last 12 hours (**recent thoughts** and **the page** below); the page is more alive at no model cost (he wanders, thought bubbles rise); the pulse and the mind are told the screen is theirs to use; desk mode is gone (the owner at the desk gets the normal stage — they often watch it); the reply card renders clean ([the /screen reply card](#the-screen-reply-card)).
- **Pieces** (pure `normalize_scene(pieces, me, why)` → `({pieces, me, why}, warnings)` in `screen_service.py`).
  - Authored: `words` (≤ 200 characters, big display type), `note` (body ≤ 1,200, a title), `list` (≤ 12 items, strings or `{label, detail}`), `stat`, `countdown` (an ISO target), `timer` (`seconds` 1–43,200 or `until`, stored as aware-UTC `started_at` / `ends_at`, capped at 12 h), `image` (`hub_canvas_service._check_url`), `video` (YouTube → `embed_url`, an https .mp4/.webm/.m4v/.mov, or a local `/api/` path), `html` (≤ 30,000 characters, sandboxed on the page).
  - Live, fetching their own data on the page: `clock`, `calendar`, `pets` (the hub care board), `alerts`, `heartbeat` (shown as Listening), `recents`, `workers`, `vitals` (labelled with this Mac's short hostname), `memory_ticker`.
  - `edward` is not a piece: he is `me`. Each piece has an `id` (`[a-z0-9_-]{1,24}`, else `p1`, `p2`…), a `place` (the nine anchors `top-left` … `bottom-right`, or `{x, y}` / `{x, y, w, h}` in cells, clamped), a `size` `xs`–`xl` from a per-kind table (`PIECE_SIZES`; `m` by default, `words` `l`), `enter` (`fade`, `rise`, `type`) and a title.
  - **A bad piece is dropped with a warning, never fatal**: a scene with one typo still lands; past eight, the rest are left off.
- **Placement** (pure `place_scene`, deterministic).
  - Edward first when he stands on the floor (4×5 on rows 7–11 at his `x` hint, default 4, moved to the nearest free x), sits in a corner (2×3) or peeks in from an edge (2×4); no room on the floor → corner → edge.
  - Then the pieces in order: their spot if free, else the free spot of that size whose centre is nearest, else one size smaller, else left off ("… did not fit and was left off"; a move of more than 4 cells and a shrink are reported too).
  - `{"on": "<piece id>"}` puts a 3×4 box above that piece's right half, or back on the floor when there is no room or no such piece. The pieces are placed before him there, so floor, corners and edges can all be taken; then he stands in the nearest free box (4×5, 3×4, 2×3, 2×4) near his floor spot, and only when nothing at all is free does he peek in from his edge over a piece ("the stage is full"). Review 2026-09-28: he had been drawn over a piece in ~7.5 % of `on` scenes with most of the stage free.
  - `look` is a piece id or "owner". **His mood is never an input**: it comes only from real signals.
  - `default_scene()` is the stock scene (the clock top-left, today's calendar top-right, the pets bottom-right, Edward on the floor at x 4); the page's `FALLBACK_SCENE` (`display/lib/stageModel.ts`, for a failed first fetch) mirrors its placement cell for cell. A stored 6×4 layout from before is converted on read (`tiles_to_scene`); nothing writes one any more.
- **The owner's ask holds the screen.** A scene composed or a stage show put up in the owner's own turn (`is_owners_turn()`: the turn's source is in `screen_service.OWNER_SOURCES` — chat, voice, widget, Alexa, their self-chat `ping_owner` and `owner_ping`, the same list as `memory_service.OWNER_SPEAKS_SOURCES`; a call outside any turn is not theirs) or through the REST routes sets `asked_at` / `asked_until`: for a scene, `hold_minutes` (default 120, at most 720); for a show, until it expires, else two hours.
  - Each ask keeps its own end (`held_until` inside the stored scene or show), and the hold is the later of the new ask's and the other ask still on the screen: a 30-second alert asked for over a scene held all evening does not cut the scene's hold, nor a short scene the hold of a show over it (review 2026-09-28). A new scene of the owner's replaces their old scene's hold.
  - While it holds, a turn that isn't the owner's gets `ScreenHeld` from `compose_screen`, `reset_screen_layout`, `show_on_screen(screen="mini")` or `clear_screen(screen="mini")`, and nothing changes; the tool answers "Not changed: <owner> asked for what is on your screen, and it holds until 8:10 pm. say_on_screen still works." The owner's own clear or reset ends the hold; otherwise it lapses at `asked_until`, and nothing is deleted.
  - The page's tap (`DELETE /api/screen/show`, `by="tap"`) neither obeys nor ends a hold, but once it takes the owner's show down over a scene that is not theirs, the page is no longer told of the hold (`asked`, below), so it never says "You asked …" under Edward's scene.
- **Lines** (`screen_service.say`, the `say_on_screen` tool): whitespace collapsed, ≤ 240 characters (cut at a word), the last six kept in `lines` as `{id, text, at, aloud}`; the snapshot carries the ones from the last 10 minutes. `aloud` is dropped during quiet hours in the owner's zone (`push_ledger`'s window, `EDWARD_QUIET_HOURS`) **and in any surplus turn** (`screen_service.in_surplus_turn`: nobody asked for that turn; no surplus turn has run since the queue was retired on 2026-10-11). `say` answers `quiet` and `quiet_why` (`surplus`, `hours` or null), and the tool says which: "Said on your screen at 5:52 pm (quietly — it's quiet hours)." A line is never held.
- **The tools.** In the toolset, every turn holds the same `screen` tool (`screen(compose | say | show | clear | reset | bring_up | widget …)`, [tool-loop.md](tool-loop.md)); what a turn may do there is its policy and the owner's hold, not a shorter list. The legacy names (`graph/tools.py`, whose docstrings are the manual):
  - `compose_screen(pieces, me, why, hold_minutes)` answers "Your screen now: words 'A quiet Monday…' top-left, image top-right, note 'A thought' bottom-left; you stand on 'thought'." — where each piece landed (its own place name when it sits exactly there, else the region its rect is in: `describe_placed`), plus "It holds until 8:10 pm because <owner> asked." in the owner's turn and "Notes: …" for the warnings, or "Not changed: …";
  - `say_on_screen(line, aloud)` answers "Said aloud on your screen at 5:52 pm.";
  - `reset_screen_layout` goes back to the stock scene; `show_on_screen`, `clear_screen`, `look_at_screen` and `bring_up_screen` as before, plus the hold. `set_screen_layout` and `screen_service.set_layout` are gone (`lib/toolLabels.ts` keeps its label for old chat receipts).
  - The mind's prompt has one line for it ("This Mac's screen (if one is set up) is yours: … `say_on_screen` it."; a mind prompt change, so the judgement check reruns before a deploy). The `compose_screen` manual says the screen is his and change is welcome, and that composing never moves a window, so it is fine while the owner is at the desk.
  - A second identical `say_on_screen` in one turn really runs (it is time-varying), and `compose_` / `say_` are mutating prefixes. The journal (`thought_journal.SURFACE_TOOLS`: "Set the scene on the Mac screen", "Said something on the Mac screen") and the pulse ledger (`event_ledger.SURFACE_TOOLS` → `screen`) count both. Neither is taint-gated, the same as `show_on_screen`; `compose_screen` does not pull the /screen tab forward (`show_on_screen` does, with hands on), so a pulse never brings Chrome to the front while the owner may be at the desk.
- **The thinking stream** (`services/screen_thoughts.py`, served by `GET /api/screen/live`, polled every 2 s). The live stream is in process and in memory: nothing is written, and a restart forgets it (his recent thoughts, below, are the durable part).
  - It follows only `mind` and `pulse` turns (`turn_ledger.SCREEN_THOUGHT_SOURCES`): the owner's own chat is on their phone, not on the wall.
  - `turn_ledger` feeds it from both tool loops through one guarded hook (`_screen`: a lazy import that never raises, so the screen can never break or slow a turn): `begin_turn` → `begin`; `note_response_reasoning` → `note_reasoning` with only the summaries that response added; a tool start → `tool_started` (the streamed loop's `tool_start` event; the non-streaming loop, where wakes and the pulse run, calls `turn_ledger.note_tool_started` just before executing the call); a tool result → `tool_finished`; `end_turn` → `end`.
  - One line per reasoning summary: markdown stripped, a bold or `#` heading dropped when a body follows, whole sentences up to 220 characters (else words and "…"), **scrubbed fail-closed** the way the chat trace is (`turn_trace._scrub` on the full summary and on the line shown: a secret, even one the cut removed, hides the whole line as "[hidden: looked like a secret]"), a repeat said once, the last 8 kept. Receipts are `{tool, state: running|ok|error, at}`: **names only, never arguments or results**, the last 12.
  - When the turn ends it folds into one line (its last line or "I looked things over.", its `about`, when, `tools` = every call it made); a turn that ended `error` or `stopped` folds to "I didn't get to finish that.", never to the stock line or its last half-finished thought (review 2026-09-28). The last three folds are kept. `about` is the wake reason, "surfaces" for the pulse.
  - **A live turn older than 20 minutes drops out**: a crashed turn must not look like thinking. The page holds the same rule on its own (`thinkingModel.LIVE_STALE_AFTER_MS`), and it drops the poll's last good answer after two failed reads in a row or while Edward is offline (`liveOnStage`, review 2026-09-28): only a poll that gets through can carry the backend's drop, so without it an outage mid-wake left "Thinking" beside an "Offline" line. For a wake the lane keeps saying Thinking through the few seconds of memory extraction after the answer (`end_turn` runs after it): true, he is still working.
- **Recent thoughts** (round 2; `screen_thoughts.recent` over the pure `screen_thoughts.recent_lines`, served as `recent` on `GET /api/screen/live`): what the lane drifts through between turns, read from durable rows so a restart keeps them.
  - The closing notes of his own thinking in `ledger_events` from the last 12 hours (`RECENT_HOURS`): `kind="mind"` rows with outcome `acted` or `left_alone` (the row's `reason` is the wake's note, `source` its reason; `surfaces` left out, and the pulse writes no mind row anyway), the nightly's closing included. Until 2026-10-11 it also read `kind="surplus"` rows; the queue is retired and those rows are no longer read. Never the pulse's decisions (they are about surfaces, not thoughts), a failed or skipped wake, or anything of the owner's chat.
  - **A `speak` wake is private** (2026-10-11): it is about something Edward noticed in the owner's messages, so its reasoning lines are never shown while it runs (its tool receipts are), and its closing, live fold or ledger row, is the fixed `screen_thoughts.SPEAK_LINE` ("Something came up — I left a note."); the claim never reaches the lane (`tests/test_screen_lane_private.py`).
  - Each is `{text, at, about, kind}`: `text` cleaned like a live line (`thought_line`), `at` aware UTC ISO, `about` the wake reason, `kind` the ledger kind (`mind`). Boilerplate is dropped ("(no note)", "Nothing to do." …), and so is a failure report that closed a wake without being a thought: `tool_policy.honest_ending`'s "I stopped before finishing: …", a `mind_service.RETRY_MARK` line echoed back, and a `Tool error:` answer (`_FAILURE_PREFIXES`). Identical notes are kept once; a line the secret scan flags is **left out** (fail-closed, with no placeholder in the ambient lane). Newest last, at most 12 (`MAX_RECENT`).
  - The read is bounded at 3 s and cached 30 s in process (`RECENT_TTL_S`; the page polls every 2 s); a failure reads as `recent: []` and is cached too, and the route never 500s whichever half fails.
  - These are wakes' closing notes, so one can name someone the owner is messaging ("Answered Rowan about dinner"). They show only on `/screen`, never on the household hub.
- **The snapshot** (`GET /api/screen`, polled every 4 s; pure `build_snapshot`): `stage` `{cols: 24, rows: 12}`; `scene` (the placed `pieces`, each with its `rect` in cells and its content fields; `me` `{at, rect, on, side, look}`; `why`; `source` — `edward`, `owner` or `default`; `updated_at`, `updated_by`); `asked` (null unless a hold is active now **and** something the owner asked for is on the page); `held_until` (the active hold whatever is showing; not drawn); `lines`; `last_said` (the newest line whatever its age; not drawn, it keeps the pulse's description stable when a line ages off); `owner_at_mini`; `show`; `now`. The old `grid` / `layout*` keys stay one release, so an old open tab keeps drawing until it reloads. A stored scene is re-normalized and re-placed on every read. `owner_at_mini` (is the owner at this Mac) is for Edward's pulse; the page uses it only to keep night mode off while the owner is there. It is the host's own idle probe (`presence_service.owner_at_mini` → pure `presence_logic.at_device`: the local Mac's beacon fresh within 90 s and touched in the last 120 s; a `display` beacon never counts — a lit kiosk is not a person).
- **The page** (`display/components/Stage.tsx`; pure `display/lib/stageModel.ts`, `stageLife.ts`, `thinkingModel.ts`; his mind's drift in `display/hooks/useMindDrift.ts`).
  - An ink canvas (#0a0e14) with the pieces placed from their cells (`rectStyle`; `AuthoredTiles.tsx` draws the authored kinds; the clock is `LiveClock` `face="stage"`; the calendar and Listening (`heartbeat`) pieces have stage faces — `StageLivePieces.tsx` over the pure `stageLive.ts`: sentence-case titles, plain words for what Listening did, the one clock format, no emerald; the other live kinds reuse the panels). Every live piece draws the title Edward gave it. Edward stands where `me` puts him (`StageEdward.tsx`, the one live Edward; `[data-edward-anchor]` and `data-hands` keep the hands-down amber tip).
  - **The lane** sits on the side of him with more free room (`laneFor`). It holds his newest line (`SpeechLane.tsx`: the overline "Said aloud · 5:52 pm" in emerald — "Said aloud" and the speaker only when the audio really started (`useSpeakAloud`'s `played`), else "Said", so a switched-off voice, a hidden tab, a failed synthesis or the browser's autoplay rule never leave a false claim; a line leads for 3 minutes, the one before it stays up to 10); else his thinking (`ThoughtLane.tsx`: a still pip, "Thinking · reading your messages" — the reason in his words through `reasonPhrase`, never raw — since when, lines fading with age, receipt chips labelled the way Chat labels tools); else **his mind** (below), where a mind turn that just ended surfaces first, large, as "Just thought · 5:47 pm" for 90 s (`thinkingModel.justThoughtOf`, `JUST_THOUGHT_MS`; never the pulse's own note); else, only when he has nothing of his mind to show, the fold's one line ("Earlier · 2:10 pm — …" for 10 minutes, `FOLD_WITHIN_MS`).
  - So the lane is empty only when he has no thought from the last 12 hours, the owner turned thinking off, the owner's ask holds the screen, or Edward is mid-walk (`laneContent`: speech > thinking > mind > fold — after the round-2 gate: with 10–20 turns an hour, a ten-minute footnote ahead of his mind was nearly always what showed). A line said while a turn runs keeps the lane for 45 s, then his thinking takes it back. What the lane crosses fades to 0.2.
  - The why receipt sits bottom-left beside an emerald dot: "I arranged this at 4:10 pm — you're out and nothing needs you.", "You set this at …" for a scene the owner set, or "You asked at 6:10 pm · stays up until 8:10 pm" while their hold is on; it hides while his thinking fills the lane. While the owner's ask holds, Edward's words stay small beside him, nothing the owner asked for dims, and neither the fold nor his mind is shown.
  - The line under Edward is his presence line (`stageStatusLine`; "· Chrome link down" stays), except "Speaking" only while his voice really plays and "Thinking" while a live turn exists.
  - `ComposeBar` and the key hints show only after the mouse or a key moves (6 s), and stay while a reply card is on the bar or the cursor is in its box (`onActiveChange`). The bar sits on a scrim, the pieces under it (`COMPOSE_RECT`) fade back, and the owner's send is neutral. **His lane lays out around the bar**: while it is up `ScreenLayout` measures what the bar and its reply card cover once a second (`stageModel.coveredCells`), live thinking ends above it (`clipAbove`), and a thought on his mind finds room clear of it. **The bar never covers Edward** (round-2 review): `stageLife.clearOfBand` steps him along the floor to the nearest spot clear of the bar and the pieces, or, with no room beside it, stands him up on the bar, his feet on its top edge; he goes back when it goes.

## Edward's hands in the owner's Chrome

**Off by default** (`EDWARD_CHROME_HANDS`, read per call; on/1/true/yes turns them on). Edward's hands act in the owner's own browser, with the owner's logins, in the owner's name, so they are opt-in. While off: the extension socket is refused (`WS /api/browser/ext` closes with 4004), `browser_hands` starts nothing, every `chrome` action and `look("chrome")` answers "Chrome hands are off — set EDWARD_CHROME_HANDS=on and load chrome-extension/ (see its README)", `screen_window_service` never launches, raises, relaunches or quits Chrome, `GET /api/browser/ext/status` reports `{enabled: false, message}` (so `/api/mind/now` reports the hands as unknown, not down), and the pulse's trigger leaves out its "Your hands in Chrome" line. `/screen` itself still works when you open it by hand.

To turn them on: set `EDWARD_CHROME_HANDS=on`, load the unpacked extension from `chrome-extension/` once (`chrome://extensions` → Developer mode → Load unpacked; `chrome-extension/README.md`; its ID is always `appfgnpjahkedldopocalbkoommcalae`, below), and grant the backend's python Automation (Chrome, System Events, Finder) and Accessibility when macOS asks.

- **Two browsers, two tools** (the toolset rebuild, 2026-10-11 — [computer.md](computer.md)): the fourteen legacy `browser_*` tools below are one `chrome` tool (`services/toolset/browsers.py`) with typing, acting-click and payment rails, and Edward has **his own invisible browser** besides: `browser`, Playwright's bundled headless shell (`services/headless_browser.py`: never Google Chrome, http(s) only, never this Mac's own services), for JavaScript pages, forms and downloads with none of the owner's logins. It works whatever `EDWARD_CHROME_HANDS` says. The hands' page actions take a `target`, so both read and click alike. `look("screen" | "chrome" | "browser")` returns the picture itself, and the Chrome picture is taken over CDP without activating the tab. The legacy tools remain only for `EDWARD_TOOLSET=legacy` and the operator route.

(`backend/services/browser_hands.py`, `backend/services/browser_ext_service.py`, `backend/routers/browser_ext.py`, `chrome-extension/`)

- **Why** (2026-09-07): Edward should be able to do in the browser what the owner can; the owner opens a tab and signs in, and he takes over. Most "put X on your screen" asks became: open a tab in the owner's Chrome and go full screen, then come back to the /screen tab when done. So there is **one browser** for acting as the owner: theirs, with their logins, on their monitor. The same evening this replaced three earlier layers, all deleted: a browser-automation skill (Lightpanda, an earlier Playwright tool, and a tier that asked the owner to launch a second Chrome with `--remote-debugging-port`), a headless Chrome LaunchAgent that screencast into a browser tile, and `kind="web"` shows. (Playwright came back in 2026-10 as Edward's own headless browser; it never drives the owner's Chrome.)
- **The extension** (`chrome-extension/`, MV3: `debugger`, `tabs`, `alarms`, `<all_urls>`) keeps one WebSocket to `ws://127.0.0.1:8000/api/browser/ext` (only from this Mac, below; the auth middleware never sees WebSockets) and relays the tab list (`tabs.list`, pushed on every change), tab and window actions (`tabs.open/activate/close/navigate/info`, `tabs.capture` = `captureVisibleTab` JPEG, `windows.fullscreen` = `chrome.windows.update(state)`), and `chrome.debugger` commands (`cdp`). After a code change: the reload arrow on the extension's card, or restart Chrome. `services/browser_ext_service.py` owns the socket (ids, the tab cache, listeners; pure `encode_call` / `decode_message` / `format_tabs`).
- **Who may be the extension** (2026-10-12; `routers/browser_ext.py::refusal`). A loopback peer alone proves nothing: Next's `/api` rewrite and a tunnel both reach uvicorn from 127.0.0.1, so the old loopback check let a LAN device, a page in another browser on this Mac, or anyone behind the tunnel become Edward's hands. With the hands on, the socket is accepted only when all three hold: **no proxy header** (`X-Forwarded-For`, `X-Forwarded-Host`, `X-Real-IP`, `Forwarded`, `CF-Connecting-IP`, checked on the raw headers; Next always adds `X-Forwarded-Host` and cloudflared `CF-Connecting-IP`), **a loopback peer**, and **`Origin: chrome-extension://<id>` with `<id>` Edward's own extension** (a web page cannot forge its Origin). `chrome-extension/manifest.json` carries a fixed public `key`, so every unpacked copy of the extension gets the same ID, `appfgnpjahkedldopocalbkoommcalae` (`routers/browser_ext.EDWARD_EXTENSION_ID`; the test checks it is the ID that key gives, and that no private key is in the folder), and by default only that ID is accepted. `EDWARD_CHROME_EXTENSION_ID` (comma-separated ids, from `chrome://extensions`) replaces it, for a fork that removes or changes the key. Before the 2026-10-12 review the ID was checked only when that variable was set, so by default any extension in any Chromium browser or profile on the Mac could take the link over, read what Edward types and feed him the pages he reads (an extension's service worker may open a WebSocket to 127.0.0.1 without host permissions). A refusal closes with 4003 and says why (proxied, not local, the wrong Origin, or another extension, whose ID the log names: "extension <id> is not Edward's (pin it with EDWARD_CHROME_EXTENSION_ID)"); "hands are off" (4004) is checked first. **The limits**: the key is public, so an extension built on purpose to impersonate Edward's gets the same ID; the pin keeps out the other extensions you install, not a deliberate clone. And a program on this Mac can forge every header, so the socket keeps out the network, the tunnel, web pages and other extensions, not this Mac's own processes. Edward's own shell is not one of them: it may not connect to port 8000 at all ([computer.md](computer.md#the-rails)). Tests: `tests/test_public_browser_ext_socket.py`, `tests/test_public_chrome_hands_flag.py`.
- **Chrome's debugging bar shows, on purpose.** While a tab is attached Chrome shows "… started debugging this browser": the honest signal that Edward is driving. `EDWARD_CHROME_SILENT_DEBUGGER=on` adds `--silent-debugger-extension-api` when Edward launches Chrome himself, which hides it (over a full-screen video, say); a Chrome you launched yourself always shows it.
- **Hands** (`browser_hands.py`): every action runs on the **working tab** (`open_page` / `use_tab` set it; a `detached` / `disconnected` event clears it). Reads are structured: page text plus numbered interactive elements tagged `data-edward-ref` in the DOM, with role, label, placeholder, value, checked and the scroll position (`format_page_read`). Actions (`click`, `type_text`, `press_key`, `scroll`, `back`, `navigate`) wait for the page to **settle** (`is_settled`: readyState complete, URL and height unchanged across two polls, ≤ 8 s) and return where the tab is. `wait_for(text|url_contains)` waits for an outcome. `set_fullscreen` is Chrome's own full screen for the tab's window (on macOS a fullscreen Space while it lasts; `release` leaves it). `release(close, back_to_screen)` = `browser_done`: leave full screen, detach or close, activate the `/screen` tab. `look(at="page"|"display")` = `look_at_screen`: `tabs.capture` of the working tab, or `screencapture` of the display (Screen Recording permission). `run_steps` runs up to 12 actions in one call and stops at the first failure (`plan_steps` validates). `describe_hands` feeds the pulse. Everything relayed is logged as `[browser]`: he is acting in the owner's name.
- **The window** (2026-09-07). The first version ran a dedicated fullscreen Chrome, which lived in its own macOS Space the owner could not reach with a mouse, and a second window opened beside the one already up. Now `screen_window_service.ensure_screen_tab()` runs AppleScript: find a Chrome tab on one of the screen hosts with `/screen`, `/display` or `/fullscreen`, else open one; leave any fullscreen Space (`AXFullScreen` off); set the window bounds to the desktop; activate. It is called in the background by `show_on_screen(screen="mini")`, on demand by `bring_up_screen` / `POST /api/screen/window`, and by `browser_done` (through the extension, which activates the screen tab).
  - Two macOS facts are baked in: AppleScript `activate` is ignored while another app has focus, so activation goes through `open -a`; and a background process cannot pull a window over from another Space (a new window opens beside it there), so when System Events lists zero Chrome windows on this Space and Chrome holds nothing but screen tabs, the service quits Chrome and relaunches it with the URL (`_relaunch_on_this_space`). With other tabs open, it leaves the window where it is and says so.
  - When Chrome is not running, the service launches it itself with `--autoplay-policy=no-user-gesture-required` (`launch_argv`): without it, a video Edward opens sits on its play button until someone clicks the page. Branded Chrome ignores `--load-extension`, so it is not passed; the unpacked extension persists in the profile.
- **Tools** (legacy registry): `browser_tabs`, `browser_open(url, fullscreen, new_tab)` (returns a first read), `browser_use_tab`, `browser_read`, `browser_find`, `browser_click`, `browser_type`, `browser_key`, `browser_scroll`, `browser_back`, `browser_wait`, `browser_fullscreen`, `browser_steps`, `browser_done`; plus `look_at_screen(at)` and `bring_up_screen`. - **Hands recovery** (2026-09-14). Chrome relaunched itself window-less after a background update, a short session closed its profile, and the process lingered with zero renderers: the extension worker was unloaded, so its 30 s reconnect alarm never fired, and `ensure_screen_tab` saw "a Chrome process" and never relaunched. That uses the pure `chrome_needs_relaunch(process_running, renderers, link_connected, link_down_s)`: relaunch only a running Chrome with zero renderer processes and the link down ≥ `RELAUNCH_AFTER_S` (60 s); a Chrome with windows is the owner's and is left alone.
