# Voice

Voice memos and live voice: ElevenLabs Scribe and streaming TTS, the socket protocol, mic sensitivity, handoffs, the hub's voice, and the presence orb.

## Voice

**The voice system** (`backend/services/voice_service.py`, `backend/services/voice/`, `backend/routers/voice.py`, `backend/routers/voice_ws.py`). It needs `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` (and `ELEVENLABS_VOICE_ID` for the voice he speaks in) and is toggled by the "voice" skill.

- **ElevenLabs end to end** (rebuilt 2026-09-05; the earlier Mistral/Voxtral path is gone): batch STT `scribe_v2` for memos, realtime STT `scribe_v2_realtime` over a WebSocket for live conversation, and TTS through the stream-input WebSocket as raw PCM at 24 kHz (`eleven_flash_v2_5` by default; `ELEVENLABS_TTS_MODEL` swaps in `eleven_v3_conversational`).
- **Two ways to talk**:
  1. The mic button in the chat input records a **voice memo** (MediaRecorder; Safari gives `audio/mp4`, Chrome `audio/webm`) and either posts it as a chat attachment or transcribes it into the text box. A posted memo is stored in file storage and transcribed server-side in `routers/chat.py::_parse_chat_request`; the transcript becomes the message text, and the audio survives as a playable attachment (a marker text block plus `kind: "voice_memo"` metadata; `routers/conversations.py::serialize_checkpoint_message` turns it back into an attachment). The chat stream leads with a `user_message` event so the bubble shows the transcript.
  2. **Live voice mode** (`ContinuousVoiceView`, `useVoiceSession`): the browser streams 16 kHz PCM16 mic frames continuously; the backend relays them to Scribe, which does voice-activity detection server-side (a 0.7 s silence commits) and streams partial captions; the committed transcript runs a normal Edward turn in the same conversation. Hands-free or hold-to-talk (`mode: "ptt"`, manual commits).
- **Mic sensitivity** (2026-09-06). Scribe's VAD hears the whole room: a TV committed "transcripts", and Edward answered them or cut himself off. Three levels in the voice view, stored in `edward-voice-sensitivity` and sent as `sensitivity` on `start_session`:
  - `high`: hears everything (the old behaviour);
  - `normal` (the default): a client-side noise gate (`lib/voice/noiseGate.ts`) tracks the room's floor and sends anything not clearly above it as silence;
  - `low` (a noisy room): a wider gate, Scribe gets `vad_threshold=0.65` and `min_speech_duration_ms=250` (`scribe_protocol.scribe_tuning`), and barge-in needs three words instead of two (`turn_state.barge_in_min_words`).

  The gate only runs hands-free; hold-to-talk is deliberate already. Gated frames are still sent (as zeros) so Scribe's silence timer keeps running.
- **Spoken turns are different on purpose.** They run on the `voice` route: Sonnet 5.5 at low effort with a one-hour prompt cache under Claude (`EDWARD_CLAUDE_VOICE_MODEL` / `_EFFORT` override it), or `EDWARD_VOICE_MODEL` (default `gpt-5.6-luna`, the fastest tier to first token) on the Codex fallback ([llm-and-quota.md](llm-and-quota.md)). `voice_system_prompt` appends spoken-style guidance, and `stream_with_memory_events(fast_context=True)` skips the speculative deep-retrieval wait and the session briefing (`_context_plan`).
- **Bridges**: when the first token is late (`BRIDGE_THRESHOLD_S`, 1.0 s), Edward speaks a randomized bridge line (`services/voice/bridges.py`, never the same one twice in a row).
- **Barge-in**: a partial transcript of two or more words while Edward is speaking cancels the turn (`services/voice/turn_state.py`); one word is treated as echo.
- **Voice keeps working** (2026-09-24): a spoken turn still running tools after `VOICE_HANDOFF_SECONDS` (12 s), or at its third tool call, says a handoff line (`services/voice/handoff.py`) and continues detached (`_DETACHED_TURNS`). Barge-in and ending the session don't cancel it; `interrupt` or a spoken "stop" / "never mind" does. The result is spoken ("Okay, that's done. …") when the session is open and quiet, held for the next quiet moment when it is busy, and pushed (`source="voice_handoff"`, `/chat?c=<id>`) when the session has ended. A hub session's result is instead a note to the owner through `reach_service` as the hub (`_leave_hub_result`, origin `hub`, ledger outcome `noted`): whoever asked there is not necessarily the owner, and a raw push skipped the reach rules. `handoff` / `handoff_done` frames drive the view's "Working in the background" line. A new question waits for it (one checkpoint, one writer). One `voice_handoff` ledger row per handoff. Tests: `tests/test_voice_handoff.py`, `tests/test_voice_ws_router.py`, `__tests__/voiceMachine.test.ts`.
- **The hub's voice is not the owner** (a security fix, 2026-10-11). `start_session` carries `surface`: `app` (the default, and anything unknown) is the chat page's live voice, the owner, `turn_source="voice"`; `hub` (sent by `/hub/ask`: `ContinuousVoiceView surface="hub"` → `voiceProtocol.startSessionMessage`) runs every turn of the session, a handed-off one included, as `hub_voice` (`voice_ws.TURN_SOURCE_BY_SURFACE`).
  - **Why**: it was `voice` before, so anyone standing at the household panel had the owner's rights; Edward would have texted anyone from the owner's number for them.
  - `hub_voice` is spoken (the voice route, prompt and `fast_context`: `claude_routing.VOICE_TURN_SOURCES`, at interactive priority) but it is someone in the owner's home, not necessarily the owner. Its default policy is the recipient rule, never texting the owner, stand-downs and the Chrome typing rule ([tool-loop.md](tool-loop.md#turnpolicy--what-a-turn-may-do-toolsetpolicypy)), and it is in no list that means the owner is speaking (pinned by `tests/test_hub_voice_source.py`).
  - Its turns, a handed-off one included, spend Edward's own share (`llm_spend.own_work_scope`, like a friend's ping): they stop at the daily cap, never in the owner's reserve. Its `voice_handoff` rows carry `source="hub_voice"` and its `voice_session` row `detail.surface`.
  - The surface is the page's word; the server cannot verify it. Tests: `tests/test_voice_ws_router.py`, `__tests__/voiceSurface.test.ts`.
- **Wire formats and every decision are pure and tested** (`services/voice/*_protocol.py`, `sentence_feed.py`, `turn_state.py`, `bridges.py`, `memo.py`; frontend `lib/voice/*`, with tests in `frontend/__tests__/voice*.test.ts`). `tests/test_voice_service.py` runs the sockets against fake local `websockets` servers; `tests/test_voice_ws_router.py` drives a whole session through FastAPI's test client (a fake Scribe emits partials and commits from `P:`/`C:` mic frames).
- **The socket protocol** is documented at the top of `routers/voice_ws.py`. Client → server: `start_session` (`mode`, `sensitivity`, `surface`, `conversation_id`), `0x01`+PCM, `commit`, `interrupt`, `end_session`. Server → client: `session_started`, `partial_transcript`, `transcript`, `state`, `bridge`, `response_text`, `tool_activity`, `0x02`+PCM, `response_done`, `error`.
- **Live voice is a presence orb** (`ContinuousVoiceView`, pure `lib/voice/voicePresence.ts`; 2026-09-26). Edward, large, is the screen's hero (it registers with `presenceHero`). His mood is a claim like everywhere else ([design-and-presence.md](design-and-presence.md)): he listens only while the mic is really open (hands-free, or the button held), thinks while the turn runs, works while a tool runs, and speaks while his audio plays, including playback that outlives the server's move back to listening (`visiblePhase`). An error is a wince, a dropped link offline, a handed-off job finishing an `acted` beat. One sonar ring follows `--level` (the mic while he listens, his own voice while he speaks) and his mouth follows `--mouth` (`lipsync`); those CSS variables are the only per-frame writes, from one rAF loop that pauses while hidden and never runs under reduced motion. Mode and mic sensitivity live in a small sheet; Stop and End are big and plain.
- `/chat?voice=1` (the manifest's "Talk live" shortcut) opens voice once and is dropped from the URL (`chatRoute.readVoiceParam` / `withoutVoiceParam`). A route change away from `/chat` ends voice (`lib/voiceRoute.ts::shouldEndVoiceOnRoute`); before, presence read "Listening" with no mic open, the queue stayed held, and /chat reopened the mic unasked. Tests: `__tests__/voicePresence.test.ts`, `voiceRoute.test.ts`, `voiceViewWiring.test.ts`, `voiceLevel.test.ts`.
- **Errors in his voice** (2026-09-27): a mic that would not start reads "I can't hear you" over a line that says what to do (`micErrorMessage` in `lib/voice/micErrors.ts`: not found, not allowed, in use by another app; re-exported by `useMicStream` and used by the chat's voice-memo button, so it does not pull in the live-voice hook). Anything else on the voice link is "I lost the voice line — tap to try again". Tests: `voicePresence.test.ts`, `micStreamErrors.test.ts`.
- **A new build waits for the microphone**: `useMicStream` holds reloads while the mic is open or opening, and a memo being recorded holds too ([app-shell-today-chat.md](app-shell-today-chat.md#new-builds-reach-open-tabs)).
- Voice turns record `completed` in `turn_log` (the socket breaks on `done`; every voice turn used to read `stopped`), and their model calls are labelled `turn:voice` (`turn:hub_voice` from the hub).
- **Frontend audio**: one shared `AudioContext` (`lib/voice/audioContext.ts`; iOS needs a tap to unlock output, and the view shows "Tap to enable sound"), an inline AudioWorklet for capture (`useMicStream`, downsampled to 16 kHz), and `usePcmPlayer`, which schedules raw PCM chunks gaplessly. No ONNX/VAD model is downloaded.
- HTTP endpoints: `GET /api/voice/status`, `POST /api/voice/transcribe` (`{text, language}`), `POST /api/voice/synthesize` (MP3).
